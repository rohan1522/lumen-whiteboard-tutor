# 06 — Evaluation framework

We review submissions holistically. The framework below helps make evaluation more consistent while leaving room for different product interpretations, technologies, and applicant strengths.

| Evaluation dimension | Weight | What we may look for |
|---|---:|---|
| Product understanding and problem framing | 15 | Clear target learner, meaningful problem, thoughtful scope, and a coherent explanation of the opportunity. |
| Originality and product thinking | 15 | Independent ideas, useful choices, attention to learner needs, and a distinctive or well-justified approach. |
| Engineering and problem-solving | 20 | Sound implementation, practical decisions, handling of edge cases, and the ability to reason through technical challenges. |
| AI integration and application | 15 | Appropriate use of AI, quality of integration, transparency about live versus simulated behaviour, and awareness of limitations. |
| Teaching and interaction quality | 15 | Clarity, usefulness, engagement, and how effectively the experience supports understanding or practice. |
| Code quality and technical decisions | 10 | Readability, organisation, maintainability appropriate to scope, and justified trade-offs. |
| Documentation and communication | 10 | Clear README, setup/review guidance, explanation of decisions, and honest disclosure of limitations. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## Interpreting scores

The following bands are broad review aids, not automatic hiring thresholds:

- **85–100:** Particularly strong evidence across the work.
- **70–84:** Promising submission with several clear strengths.
- **50–69:** Mixed evidence; some strengths alongside notable gaps.
- **Below 50:** Limited evidence against the dimensions in this framework.

## Review principles

- We do not reward feature count for its own sake.
- A focused, complete product can score strongly.
- We do not require a particular framework, model provider, architecture, or visual style.
- We consider the quality of reasoning and execution in the context of the chosen scope.
- Honest communication about limitations is better than presenting simulated functionality as live.
- Scores inform review; they do not independently determine interview or employment decisions.
